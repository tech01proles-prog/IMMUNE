# 4. itch.io Page Copy — English

## Section: Intro

Immune Empire: Internal Front is an incremental strategy game about building a living defense civilization inside a human body.

You do not click monsters or manage a factory. You command an immune system.

Digest nutrients, grow stem cells, train leukocytes, contain bacterial and viral outbreaks, balance inflammation, unlock adaptive immunity, collect antigens, create vaccines, and ascend to a new host with permanent immune memory.

Every defeated pathogen becomes knowledge. Every new host makes your empire stronger.

## Section: What makes it different

Most incremental games are about buying more generators.

Immune Empire is about ecological balance.

- Too little defense? Outbreaks overwhelm your organs.
- Too much inflammation? Your own tissues suffer.
- Too narrow an army? Pathogens adapt.
- Too little regulation? Autoimmune flares begin.
- Too little energy? Even the strongest cells slow down.

You can develop many viable strategies:

- Swarm Sovereign: mass neutrophils, aggressive innate response, fast bacterial suppression.
- Adaptive Archivist: dendritic cells, lymph nodes, B/T cells, antibodies, immune memory.
- Barrier Gardener: skin, mucosa, microbiome, competitive exclusion, fewer incoming infections.
- Metabolic Warlord: mitochondria, ATP, glucose, fever, sustained high-energy defense.
- Tolerance Diplomat: regulatory T cells, resolution mediators, stability, long-term survival.
- Viral Hunter: NK cells, interferons, cytotoxic T cells, surveillance against hidden infections.

There is no single “best” build. The body is an ecosystem, and you are its strategist.

## Section: Core loop

1. Organs produce nutrients: amino acids, glucose, lipids, micronutrients.
2. Mitochondria convert fuel into ATP.
3. Bone marrow creates stem cells.
4. Stem cells train immune defenders.
5. Pathogens appear as outbreaks in sectors and organs.
6. Your cells automatically fight infections.
7. Victories yield antigens.
8. Antigens unlock adaptive immunity, vaccines and codex entries.
9. When ready, reset through a New Host prestige and keep immune memory.

## Section: Features

- Incremental idle gameplay with biological strategy depth.
- Multiple immune branches: innate, adaptive, barrier, metabolic, regulatory, specialized warfare.
- Pathogen variety: bacteria, viruses, fungi, parasites, toxins, altered self cells, autoimmune events.
- Immune memory and codex collection.
- Vaccines and meta-progression.
- Prestige system: ascend to new host archetypes.
- Offline progress.
- Clean web-friendly UI.
- Stylized biomedical sci-fi art direction.
- No aggressive pay-to-win. Optional rewarded ads or donations only.

## Section: How to play

- Buy organs to produce resources.
- Train immune cells to raise defense.
- Watch infection bars and pathogen types.
- Match your army to the threat:
  - bacteria prefer innate phagocytes;
  - viruses need NK and cytotoxic responses;
  - fungi require sustained pressure;
  - parasites need specialized barrier/Th2 tools;
  - toxins need antibodies and detox.
- Keep ATP income above cell upkeep.
- Balance inflammation and tolerance.
- Collect antigens to unlock adaptive technologies.
- Reset via New Host when you want permanent memory bonuses.

## Section: Good for players who like

- idle/incremental games;
- resource management;
- biological simulations;
- tech trees;
- strategy without twitch reflexes;
- collecting codex entries;
- prestige loops;
- weird science themes;
- clean web games.

## Section: Current status

Early access / playable web demo.

The current build focuses on the core immune loop: nutrients, organs, innate cells, early viruses, antigens, basic technologies and first prestige.

More pathogens, host archetypes, vaccines, microbiome mechanics and endgame synthetic immunity are planned.

## Section: Support / feedback

Feedback is very welcome. Especially:

- first 10 minutes clarity;
- infection difficulty;
- resource pacing;
- which build you tried;
- whether prestige feels rewarding.

# 11. FAQ

## English FAQ

Q: Is this a medical simulator?
A: No. It is a stylized incremental strategy inspired by immunology. Real biology is simplified for gameplay clarity.

Q: Do I need to click constantly?
A: No. The game supports idle and offline progress. Active play helps early on and during special events.

Q: Can I lose permanently?
A: No. Bad balance causes setbacks, organ debuffs, inflammation damage or autoimmune flares, but not save deletion.

Q: Are there multiple builds?
A: Yes. Innate swarm, adaptive memory, barriers, metabolism, regulation and antiviral specialization are all viable.

Q: Does prestige erase everything?
A: No. Immune memory, codex, vaccines, permanent upgrades and unlocks persist.

Q: Is AI art used?
A: Yes, AI-assisted concept/icon generation may be used, followed by manual selection, cropping, palette normalization and integration. Final art direction is human-curated.

Q: Will it be free?
A: The web demo is intended to be free. Optional donations or rewarded ads may support development.

# 14. Devlog Snippets

## Devlog 1: Concept

Working title: Immune Empire: Internal Front.

The idea is an incremental game where the player manages an immune system as a living civilization. Instead of generic generators, the production chain is biological: nutrients, ATP, stem cells, leukocytes, antigens, immune memory.

The key twist is balance. More defense is not always better. Inflammation increases power but damages tissues. Narrow armies allow pathogens to adapt. Lack of regulation causes autoimmune events.

The game is being built in Godot for PC/web, targeting itch.io and CrazyGames.

## Devlog 2: Why this is not just another clicker

Classic incremental games reward linear scaling: buy more, get more.

Immune Empire wants to reward ecological tuning:

- feed the army with ATP;
- match cell types to pathogen types;
- use inflammation as a risky multiplier;
- invest in tolerance to survive your own success;
- collect antigens to turn enemies into knowledge.

The fantasy is not “I clicked faster.” It is “I understood the organism.”

## Devlog 3: Art pipeline with limited drawing skills

Since I am not a strong artist, the game uses icon/card-based biomedical sci-fi visuals instead of character animation.

Plan:
1. Generate candidate icons/cards with Qwen Image 3.0.
2. Select readable silhouettes.
3. Remove backgrounds.
4. Normalize palette and outline in post.
5. Animate simply in Godot with pulses, particles and shaders.

The goal is coherence, not hand-drawn complexity.

## Devlog 4: Web export priorities

For itch.io/CrazyGames, the build must be lightweight and iframe-safe.

Priorities:
- local save with fallback;
- offline progress capped;
- pause on hidden tab;
- large touch targets;
- release export;
- limited particles;
- no SDK dependency for first demo;
- optional rewarded ads later.

The core loop must be fun even with colored rectangles.

# 32. Final Tone Guide

Keep the store copy:
- clear;
- curious;
- slightly scientific;
- not too medical;
- not too grim;
- not childish;
- honest about early access;
- focused on the unique balance mechanic.

Avoid:
- overpromising full simulation;
- claiming medical accuracy;
- saying “realistic immune system” without caveat;
- too much jargon in first paragraph;
- aggressive monetization language;
- long tutorial text on store page.

Best hook:

You are not building a factory. You are building an immune civilization.
